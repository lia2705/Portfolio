Orcam MyEye vs
Ray - Ban Meta

a usability study

Role: User study concept, user tests, user tests evaluation

Tools: OrCam MyEye, Ray-Ban Meta

Team: Group Project

This study compares the Ray-Ban Meta and OrCam MyEye smart glasses in terms of efficiency, effectiveness, and user experience for people with visual impairments. Across realistic use cases, the Ray-Ban Meta achieved faster task completion times, higher success rates, and more positive UEQ evaluations. The OrCam MyEye offers specialized assistive features but showed major weaknesses such as limited battery life, slow response times, and unclear feedback during operation. Overall, the Ray-Ban Meta performed as the more user-friendly and versatile device, while the OrCam only matched it in perceived originality.

01

Problem Statement

People with visual impairments face numerous challenges in everyday life, particularly when it comes to orientation, reading text, and perceiving their surroundings. Modern technologies can help overcome some of these obstacles. The OrCam MyEye and the Ray-Ban Meta represent two different approaches to smart glasses:

  • OrCam MyEye was developed specifically for visually impaired individuals and uses AI-based software that processes visual information in real time and converts it into auditory signals, enabling users to live more independently.
  • Ray-Ban Meta combines lifestyle features such as a camera and audio system with an AI assistant that can answer questions or describe the environment, targeting a broader user group.

Both glasses offer features that could support people with visual impairments. The OrCam provides specialized assistance tailored to this target group, while the Ray-Ban Meta’s AI assistant could also be used for similar tasks. This project investigates whether the Meta glasses can offer support comparable to the OrCam, particularly regarding everyday usability and user-friendliness.

A total of 15 participants with artificially simulated visual impairment were recruited. The evaluation was structured around three practical use cases:

  • Sorting shapes
  • Retrieving information from documents
  • Navigating a room

Participants completed all tasks using both smart glasses after a short introduction phase. Performance was measured using task completion time (efficiency), success rate (effectiveness), and the User Experience Questionnaire (UEQ). Limitations of the study included the young and fairly homogeneous participant group.

Results

The Ray-Ban Meta glasses showed fast response times, intuitive operation, and flexible AI-driven descriptions, though occasional misunderstandings or misinterpretations occurred.
The OrCam MyEye provided relevant assistive features such as text reading and object recognition, but struggled with long response delays, unclear interaction feedback, mode-switching difficulties, and very short battery life. These factors noticeably reduced usability during testing.
The quantitative evaluation revealed clear differences between the devices:

  • Efficiency: Ray-Ban Meta significantly faster (351s) compared to OrCam MyEye (632.2s).
  • Effectiveness: Higher success rates with the Meta glasses, especially for reading and information tasks.
  • User Experience: Meta scored positively across almost all UEQ categories; OrCam only matched its rating in the category “Originality.”

Overall, the results indicate that the Ray-Ban Meta offers higher usability and a more positive user experience, while the OrCam MyEye still faces major limitations in responsiveness and workflow clarity.
